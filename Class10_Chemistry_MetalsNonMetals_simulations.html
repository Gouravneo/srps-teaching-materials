🪙 Displacement Reaction — Fe + CuSO₄ NCERT Activity 3.4 · Reactivity Series 📋 Slides 🧩 Interactive
Experiment Steps
Beaker contains blue CuSO₄ solution. Fe is more reactive than Cu — it will displace Cu from solution.
Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
Try also:
Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
Cu + FeSO₄ → No reaction
(Cu less reactive than Fe)
⚠️ The solution colour change from blue → green proves the reaction has occurred (FeSO₄ is green/pale).
⚛️ Ionic Bond Formation — NaCl NCERT Section 3.4 · Bonding 📋 Slides 🧩 Interactive
🧪 Follow the Steps
Na (2,8,1) has 1 outer electron to give.
Cl (2,8,7) needs 1 more electron.

Watch the electrons orbit, then click Move Closer!
Rule: Metals LOSE electrons → cations (+)
Non-metals GAIN electrons → anions (−)

Octet rule: All atoms want 8 outer electrons (stable noble gas configuration)
🔥 Magnesium Burning in Air NCERT Activity 3.1 · Metal + Oxygen 📋 Slides 🧩 Interactive
Experiment Controls
Mg ribbon held in tongs over a Bunsen burner. Warning: never look directly at the flame — it can damage eyes.
Products:
🔥 Bright white/dazzling flame
⚪ White powder (MgO)
The white MgO is a basic oxide — dissolves in water to form Mg(OH)₂
⚠️ MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ (base)
Turns red litmus paper BLUE
🦀 Rusting Experiment — Conditions for Corrosion NCERT Activity 3.9 · Corrosion 📋 Slides 🧩 Interactive
Experiment Setup
Three test tubes with iron nails. Watch what happens over time. Which conditions cause rusting?
Tube A: Iron nail + Dry air (cotton stopper — keeps moisture out)
Tube B: Iron nail + Boiled water (sealed — O₂ removed)
Tube C: Iron nail + Normal water + Air (both O₂ and moisture present)
4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O
(Rust = hydrated iron oxide)
⚠️ Conclusion: Rusting needs BOTH oxygen AND moisture. Either alone is not enough.
