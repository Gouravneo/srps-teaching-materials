🗺 The Big Picture — Extraction of Metals
From ore to pure metal — the complete NCERT roadmap
1
The Reactivity Series — Metal Activity Ladder
Key Concept
📌The higher the metal in the series, the more reactive it is, and the harder it is to extract from its ore.
🟣High activity (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al) — extracted by electrolysis of molten compounds.
🔵Mid activity (Zn, Fe, Sn, Pb, Cu) — extracted by reduction with carbon/CO after roasting.
🟡Low activity (Hg, Ag, Au) — found as free elements or extracted by simple heating.
2
From Ore to Metal — General Steps
Important Terms
🪨Ore = naturally occurring mineral from which a metal can be profitably extracted.
🗑Gangue = unwanted rocky/earthy impurities mixed with the ore.
🔥Roasting = heating a sulphide ore in excess air → metal oxide + SO₂.
♨️Calcination = heating a carbonate ore → metal oxide + CO₂.
⬇️Reduction = removing oxygen from oxide to get the pure metal.

🟡 Low Activity Metals — Hg, Ag, Au
At the bottom of the reactivity series — least reactive — easiest to extract
L1
Why are they "Low Activity"?
Why Easy to Extract?
⚡Low activity metals have low affinity for oxygen. Their oxides are unstable and decompose easily on heating.
🌍Gold and Silver are sometimes found as native elements (pure form) in nature because they don't react with air/water.
🌡️Mercury is found as Cinnabar (HgS) — just roast in air → HgO forms → decomposes → liquid mercury drips out!
L2
Mercury Extraction from Cinnabar (HgS)
Step-by-Step
1️⃣Ore: Cinnabar (HgS) — a red mineral, found in volcanic rocks.
2️⃣Roasting: HgS heated in air → HgO (mercury oxide) + SO₂ (sulphur dioxide gas).
3️⃣Decomposition: HgO is unstable! On continued heating it decomposes → Hg (vapour) + O₂.
4️⃣Condensation: Hg vapour cools in a condenser → liquid mercury drips out (mercury is liquid at room temperature!).
2HgS + 3O₂ → 2HgO + 2SO₂
2HgO → 2Hg + O₂ ↑
No carbon neededGravity + heating only
L3
Gold and Silver — Native / Simple Extraction
Key Facts
🌟Gold is the most unreactive metal. Found as shiny nuggets in riverbeds and rocks — it has NOT reacted with anything for millions of years!
🥈Silver sometimes occurs as native silver. Its ore (Ag₂S) can be reduced by displacement: add more reactive metal like Zn or Cu.
📌NCERT Key point: Metals below hydrogen in the reactivity series are found as free elements (native state) in nature.
Au: Native elementAg: Native or Ag₂S oreNo carbon needed
🔵 Mid Activity Metals — Zn, Fe, Cu, Pb, Sn
Moderate reactivity — extracted by carbon/CO reduction after roasting or calcination
M1
Roasting vs Calcination — Step 3 Details
Remember the Difference
🔥Roasting = sulphide ore + excess air → oxide + SO₂. Temperature below melting point.
♨️Calcination = carbonate ore + limited air → oxide + CO₂. No sulphur dioxide produced.
🎯Both processes convert the ore to a metal oxide — which is then reduced in the next step.
Roasting: ZnS, Cu₂S, FeS₂Calcination: ZnCO₃, FeCO₃
M2
Zinc Extraction — Zinc Blende (ZnS)
Complete Process
1️⃣Ore: Zinc Blende (ZnS) — concentrated by froth flotation.
2️⃣Roasting: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂ (sulphide → oxide).
3️⃣Reduction with Carbon: ZnO + C → Zn + CO₂ at 1200°C.
4️⃣Zinc vapour condenses to liquid zinc, then solidifies to pure zinc metal.
2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂ (roasting)
2ZnO + C → 2Zn + CO₂↑ (reduction)
M3
Blast Furnace — Iron Extraction from Haematite (Fe₂O₃)
Blast Furnace Reactions
📥Charge: Haematite (Fe₂O₃) + coke (C) + limestone (CaCO₃) fed from the top.
💨Hot air blast from the bottom ignites coke → CO₂ → CO (reducing agent).
C + O₂ → CO₂
CO₂ + C → 2CO (reducing agent)
Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
🗑Limestone → slag: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂; CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag) — removes gangue.
⬇️Molten iron (pig iron) sinks to the bottom and is tapped. Slag floats above iron and is tapped separately.
Pig iron: 4% carbonSlag: CaSiO₃1500°C at base

Copper Extraction from Copper Pyrites (CuFeS₂)
Auto-Reduction — Unique to Copper!
🌟Auto-reduction: Cu₂O reacts with Cu₂S directly — copper reduces itself! No carbon needed.
🫧Blister copper (98% pure) — called "blister" because SO₂ bubbles trapped inside create a blistered surface.
⚡Further purified to 99.9% copper by electrolytic refining (see Refining tab).

🟣 High Activity Metals — K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al
Most reactive — carbon CANNOT reduce their oxides — must use electrolysis
H1
Why Can't Carbon Reduce Highly Active Metal Oxides?
The Core Principle
⚡When a metal is MORE reactive than carbon, carbon CANNOT displace it. Electricity is used instead to force the reduction.
🔵At cathode (−): metal ions gain electrons → metal atom deposited (reduction).
🔴At anode (+): non-metal ions lose electrons → non-metal released (oxidation).
Na from NaClAl from Al₂O₃Mg from MgCl₂
H2
Electrolysis of Molten NaCl — Sodium Extraction
Half Reactions (Must Know!)
At Cathode (−): Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na ↓ (reduction)
At Anode (+): 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂↑ + 2e⁻ (oxidation)
⚠️NaCl must be molten (not dissolved in water) — in water, H₂ forms at cathode instead of Na.
🌡️NaCl melts at 801°C. CaCl₂ is added as flux to lower the melting point to ~600°C.
☢️Cl₂ gas is toxic — collected separately and used to make HCl, disinfectants, PVC.
H3
Hall-Héroult Process — Aluminium from Al₂O₃ (Bauxite)
Hall-Héroult Process Details
At Cathode (−): Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al (reduction)
At Anode (+): 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻ (oxidation)
❄️Cryolite (Na₃AlF₆) is added to dissolve Al₂O₃ and lower its melting point from 2050°C to ~950°C — saves huge energy costs.
🔥Carbon anodes burn away — O₂ from anode reacts with carbon: C + O₂ → CO₂. Anodes must be replaced regularly.
⚡Aluminium production is energy-intensive — this is why recycling aluminium is so important (saves 95% energy!).
Ore: Bauxite → AluminaCryolite as fluxCathode: steel lining
H4
Thermite Reaction — Al as a Reducing Agent
Thermite Reaction — NCERT Important!
⚡Al is more reactive than Fe, so Al displaces Fe from Fe₂O₃ — this is a displacement reaction.
🌡️Releases enormous heat (~2500°C) — enough to melt iron! The molten iron fills gaps in railway tracks (thermite welding).
📝NCERT exam question: "Name the reaction used in welding railway tracks." — Answer: Thermite reaction.
Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe + heat
Al displaces Fe~2500°C heatRailway track welding
✨ Refining of Metals
Getting pure metal from impure extracted metal
R1
Electrolytic Refining — The Final Purification Step
How Electrolytic Refining Works
🔴Anode (+): impure copper dissolves into the electrolyte as Cu²⁺ ions. Impurities (Zn, Fe, Ni) also dissolve.
🟢Cathode (−): only Cu²⁺ ions deposit as pure copper (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu). Less reactive impurities (Ag, Au, Pt) do NOT deposit — they fall as anode mud below the anode.
💎Anode mud contains precious metals (Ag, Au, Pt) — recovered and sold separately! This is how gold is found near copper refineries.
📌Used for: Cu (copper wire), Al, Zn, Ni. Purity achieved: 99.9%+.
Anode: impure metal dissolvesCathode: pure metal depositsAnode mud: Ag, Au, Pt
R2
Corrosion — The Reverse of Extraction!
Corrosion Concepts
🔄Corrosion is the reverse of extraction! We work hard to extract metals, then they slowly return to their oxide/compound form.
🌧️Rusting needs BOTH oxygen AND water. Iron in dry air does NOT rust. Iron in water with no O₂ does NOT rust.
🔵Galvanising: iron coated with zinc. Even if Zn layer scratches, Zn is more reactive — Zn corrodes first, protecting iron (sacrificial protection).
⚗️Alloys resist corrosion: Stainless steel (Fe+Cr+Ni) — Cr₂O₃ forms a protective layer.
Rust = Fe₂O₃·xH₂OGalvanise = Zn on FeAlloy = mix of metals
