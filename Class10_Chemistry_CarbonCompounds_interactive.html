Why Carbon is Special
Two unique properties — tetravalency and catenation — explain everything
👆 Click to explore
4️⃣
Tetravalency
Carbon has 4 electrons in its outer shell. It needs 4 more to be stable.

Carbon's electronic configuration is 2, 4.

It needs 4 more electrons to complete its outer shell (need 8 total).

It can't easily gain 4 electrons (would become C⁴⁻ — very unstable)
It can't easily lose 4 electrons (needs too much energy)

Solution: Share electrons → Covalent bonds!

Carbon forms exactly 4 bonds with other atoms → called tetravalency (tetra = 4).

CH₄ — carbon makes 4 bonds, one with each H
👆 Click to explore
🔗
Catenation
Carbon atoms can bond with each other to form chains, branches, and rings.

Catenation = ability of an element to bond with its own atoms.

Carbon does this better than any other element because:

  • C–C bonds are very stable
  • Carbon can form chains of hundreds of atoms
  • Can form straight chains, branched chains, and rings

Silicon (Si) also shows some catenation but C–C bonds are much stronger than Si–Si.

Result: Over 10 million carbon compounds known! No other element comes close.

How carbon bonds with other atoms:

👆 Click
Single Bond (—)
Share 1 pair of electrons. Found in all alkanes.
Example: CH₄ (methane) — C forms 4 single bonds with H
Single bonds are stronger than double bonds individually but less reactive.
👆 Click
Double Bond (=)
Share 2 pairs of electrons. Found in alkenes.
Example: CH₂=CH₂ (ethene)
More reactive than single bond. Can undergo addition reactions.
👆 Click
Triple Bond (≡)
Share 3 pairs of electrons. Found in alkynes.
Example: CH≡CH (ethyne / acetylene)
Most reactive. Burns with an extremely hot flame → used in welding.
Allotropes of Carbon
Same element, different arrangement → completely different properties! Click each to compare.
👆 Click
💎
Diamond
Hardest natural substance on Earth
Structure: Each C bonded to 4 C → 3D tetrahedral network

Properties:
✅ Hardest natural substance
✅ Transparent, high refractive index
❌ Does NOT conduct electricity (no free electrons)

Uses: Cutting tools, drilling, jewellery, industrial abrasives

Why so hard? Each C has 4 bonds → rigid 3D cage → nothing can scratch it!
👆 Click
✏️
Graphite
Soft, slippery, conducts electricity
Structure: Each C bonded to 3 C in hexagonal layers
Layers held by weak forces → can slide!

Properties:
✅ Soft, good lubricant
✅ Good electrical conductor (free electrons between layers)
✅ Opaque, grey-black

Uses: Pencil leads, electrode in electrolysis, lubricant, carbon brushes in motors

Why does it conduct? The 4th electron of each C is free to move between layers!
👆 Click
⚽
Fullerene (C₆₀)
Soccer ball-shaped carbon molecule
Structure: 60 C atoms in soccer ball shape
Contains 20 hexagons + 12 pentagons

Properties:
✅ Hollow cage structure
✅ Can trap other atoms/molecules inside

Uses: Nanotechnology, drug delivery (carry medicine to cancer cells), superconductors

Named after Buckminster Fuller, an architect who designed geodesic dome buildings with similar shapes!
🤔 Big Question: Diamond doesn't conduct electricity but graphite does. Both are pure carbon. Why?
→ Diamond: each C uses all 4 electrons for bonds → no free electrons.
→ Graphite: each C uses only 3 electrons for bonds → 4th electron is free to conduct!

Addition Reaction
Adding atoms across a double/triple bond. Unsaturated → saturated.
CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ →(Ni)→ CH₃–CH₃

Ethene + Hydrogen → Ethane (using Nickel catalyst)

Hydrogenation = adding H₂ across a double bond

Industrial use: Vegetable oils (liquid, unsaturated) + H₂ → Vanaspati ghee (solid, saturated)

Also add halogens: CH₂=CH₂ + Cl₂ → CH₂Cl–CH₂Cl

Only possible with unsaturated compounds! Saturated compounds can't do addition.
👆 Click
🔄
Substitution Reaction
Replace one atom with another. Saturated compounds undergo this.
CH₄ + Cl₂ →(hν)→ CH₃Cl + HCl

Methane + Chlorine → Chloromethane + Hydrogen chloride

hν = sunlight (photochemical reaction)

One H in methane is replaced by Cl

Can continue: CH₃Cl → CH₂Cl₂ → CHCl₃ → CCl₄

Saturated compounds undergo substitution (can't do addition — no double bond).
👆 Click
🔴
Oxidation
Adding oxygen or removing hydrogen from a compound.
C₂H₅OH + [O] → CH₃COOH

Ethanol → Ethanoic acid using KMnO₄ or K₂Cr₂O₇

These are oxidising agents — they provide oxygen

Colour change: KMnO₄ (purple) → colourless on reduction

This is the same process that happens when wine turns to vinegar — ethanol in wine gets oxidized to ethanoic acid!
👆 Click
🌸
Esterification
Acid + Alcohol → Ester + Water. Esters have fruity smells!
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O

Ethanoic acid + Ethanol ⇌ Ethyl ethanoate + Water

Catalyst: conc. H₂SO₄ (acid catalyst)

⇌ means reversible — ester can also break back down with water (hydrolysis)

Ethyl ethanoate smells like nail polish / pineapple! Used in perfumes and artificial flavours.

Physical Properties
What does ethanol look, smell, feel like?
  • Colourless liquid at room temperature
  • Characteristic pleasant smell
  • Boiling point: 78°C (much lower than water's 100°C)
  • Completely miscible with water (mixes in all proportions)
  • Lower density than water
  • Burns with a blue flame
👆 Click
⚗️
Chemical Reactions
What reactions does ethanol undergo?

1. Combustion:

C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O

2. With Na metal:

2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂↑

3. Oxidation to ethanoic acid:

C₂H₅OH + [O] → CH₃COOH

4. Dehydration (conc. H₂SO₄, 170°C):

C₂H₅OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O
👆 Click
🔧
Uses
Where is ethanol used in real life?
  • Antiseptic: 70% ethanol solution kills bacteria (wound cleaning)
  • Solvent: dissolves varnishes, perfumes, medicines
  • Fuel: mixed with petrol (flex fuel) — cleaner than pure petrol
  • Beverages: beer, wine, spirits (fermentation of sugars)
  • Thermometers: coloured ethanol (doesn't freeze as easily as water)
👆 Click
⚠️
Harmful Effects
What happens when ethanol is consumed?
  • Affects the brain → slows thinking, coordination
  • Long-term: damages liver (cirrhosis)
  • Addictive substance
  • Causes road accidents (drunk driving)
⚠️ Denatured alcohol: Methanol or other poisons added to ethanol to make it undrinkable (industrial use). Methanol causes permanent blindness even in small amounts!

Quick Quiz
Test what you've learnt — click your answer
1. Carbon forms covalent bonds because it has _____ electrons in its outer shell and cannot easily lose or gain all 4.
2 electrons
4 electrons
6 electrons
8 electrons
Carbon's configuration is 2,4 — 4 electrons in the outer shell. It needs 4 more to complete the shell. Gaining or losing 4 electrons requires too much energy, so it shares instead (covalent bond).
2. Which of the following is an UNSATURATED hydrocarbon?
Methane (CH₄)
Ethane (C₂H₆)
Ethene (CH₂=CH₂)
Propane (C₃H₈)
Ethene has a C=C double bond → unsaturated. Methane, Ethane, and Propane all have only single bonds → saturated (alkanes).
3. The property of carbon atoms to bond with each other is called:
Tetravalency
Catenation
Isomerism
Polymerisation
Catenation = ability to bond with its own atoms. This is what allows carbon to form long chains, branches and rings. Tetravalency = ability to form 4 bonds (with any atom).
4. 3–4% solution of ethanoic acid in water is commonly known as:
Baking soda
Ethanol
Vinegar
Glacial acetic acid
Vinegar is 3–4% ethanoic acid (acetic acid) in water. Glacial acetic acid is the pure (100%) form which freezes at 17°C. Ethanol is C₂H₅OH (alcohol, not an acid).
5. In a micelle, the hydrophobic tails of soap molecules point:
Outward toward water
Inward toward the grease/oil
In random directions
They dissolve in water directly
Hydrophobic tails "hate" water and "love" grease → they point inward, surrounding the grease droplet. Hydrophilic heads "love" water → they point outward. The micelle is then water-soluble and washes away!
