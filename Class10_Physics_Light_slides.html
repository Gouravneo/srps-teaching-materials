Nature of Light
Light travels in a straight line
Demonstrated by shadows, eclipses, and pinhole cameras.
  • Speed of light: 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum
  • Electromagnetic wave (no medium needed)
  • Can reflect, refract, diffract, interfere
  • Exhibits both wave and particle nature
  • Visible spectrum: 400 nm – 700 nm
Refractive Index
n = c / v
n = Speed in vacuum / Speed in medium
Where c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, v = speed in medium
Mediumn (approx.)
Air / Vacuum1.00
Water1.33
Glass1.50
Diamond2.42
Laws of Reflection
Two Laws
  • Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection
    ∠i = ∠r
  • The incident ray, the normal, and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane
Key Terms
Normal⊥ to surface at point of incidence
∠iBetween incident ray & normal
∠rBetween reflected ray & normal
Types of Reflection
  • Regular (Specular) — Smooth surface; parallel rays remain parallel (mirrors)
  • Diffuse — Rough surface; rays scatter in all directions (walls, paper)
Remember
Angles are always measured from the Normal, not from the surface. The normal is perpendicular (90°) to the surface.

New Cartesian Sign Convention
− All distances measured against incident ray direction (left) are negative
+ All distances measured with incident ray direction (right) are positive
− Concave mirror focal length: f < 0
+ Convex mirror focal length: f > 0
u Object distance always negative (object in front)

v = image distance (−ve = real, +ve = virtual)
f = focal length (−ve concave, +ve convex)
m = −v/u = h'/h
m < 0 → Inverted image (real)
m > 0 → Erect image (virtual)

Mirror Uses in Daily Life
🔵 Concave Mirror Uses
  • Shaving / make-up mirrors — virtual, erect, enlarged image
  • Dentists' mirror — enlarged view of teeth
  • Concave dish in solar furnace — focuses heat
  • Headlights, torches, searchlights — parallel beam
  • Astronomical telescopes (reflectors)
🟡 Convex Mirror Uses
  • Rear-view mirrors in vehicles — wider field of view, always upright image
  • Security mirrors in shops — view large area
  • Rear-view mirrors on motorcycles & trucks
  • Road bends — convex mirrors placed at blind corners
Why convex for rear-view?
Always forms virtual, erect, diminished image → wider field of view than a plane mirror of the same size.
Refraction of Light
Definition
Bending of light when it passes from one transparent medium into another due to change in speed.
Snell's Law
n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂
n₁, n₂ = refractive indices
θ₁ = angle of incidence
θ₂ = angle of refraction
Rules of Refraction
  • Rarer → Denser (e.g. air → glass): bends toward normal, θ₂ < θ₁
  • Denser → Rarer (e.g. glass → air): bends away from normal, θ₂ > θ₁
  • Normal incidence (θ₁ = 0): no bending
Everyday Examples
  • Pencil appears bent in water
  • Pool appears shallower than actual depth
  • Stars twinkle (atmospheric refraction)
  • Advance sunrise / delayed sunset
Total Internal Reflection (TIR)
Conditions for TIR
  • Light must travel from denser → rarer medium
  • Angle of incidence must exceed the Critical Angle (C)
sin C = n₂/n₁ = 1/n (glass-air)
For glass (n=1.5): C ≈ 42°
Applications of TIR
  • Optical fibres — data transmission at speed of light; TIR keeps light inside the fibre
  • Endoscope — medical imaging; flexible fibre bundles
  • Mirage — hot road appears wet; layers of hot air refract and TIR sunlight
  • Diamonds sparkle — high refractive index (2.42) gives small critical angle (~24°)
Optical Fibre — How it Works
Light enters one end of a glass fibre and undergoes repeated TIR along the walls until it exits at the other end. No light escapes — extremely efficient. Used in internet cables, medical scopes, decorative lights.

Concave Lens — Always
  • Virtual, erect, diminished image
  • Image between F and O (same side as object)
  • Used to correct myopia (short-sightedness)
  • f is always negative
Power of a Lens
P = 1/f
f in metres, P in Dioptres (D)
Key Points
Unit of PowerDioptre (D)
Convex lensP > 0 (positive)
Concave lensP < 0 (negative)
Shorter fGreater power
Combined Lenses
P = P₁ + P₂ + P₃ + ...
Total power of lenses in contact is the algebraic sum of individual powers.
Examples
f = +50 cm = +0.5 m → P = +2 D
f = −25 cm = −0.25 m → P = −4 D
f = +20 cm = +0.2 m → P = +5 D
Spectacle Lens Prescription
Optometrists prescribe lenses in Dioptres. A prescription of +2 D means a convex lens of focal length 50 cm (corrects hypermetropia). A prescription of −3 D means a concave lens of focal length 33 cm (corrects myopia).
The Human Eye
Parts & Functions
CorneaOuter transparent layer; refracts light
IrisControls pupil size (aperture)
PupilOpening; adjusts light intake

RetinaScreen; rods & cones detect light

Accommodation
Ciliary muscles change the focal length of the eye lens by squeezing or relaxing it — allowing the eye to focus on both near and far objects.
Range of Vision
Near Point25 cm (least distance of distinct vision)
Far PointInfinity (normal eye)
Image on Retina
Image formed is real, inverted — brain interprets it as upright.
Defects of Vision & Correction
Myopia
(Short-sightedness)
  • Cannot see distant objects clearly
  • Image forms in front of retina
  • Far point moves closer than infinity
  • Correction: Concave lens (−ve power)
Hypermetropia
(Far-sightedness)
  • Cannot see near objects clearly
  • Image forms behind retina
  • Near point moves beyond 25 cm
  • Correction: Convex lens (+ve power)
Presbyopia
(Age-related)
  • Both near and far vision affected
  • Ciliary muscles weaken with age
  • Lens loses flexibility
  • Correction: Bifocal lenses
Astigmatism
Cornea is not perfectly spherical — objects appear blurred or distorted. Corrected with cylindrical lenses.
Dispersion of Light
What is Dispersion?
Splitting of white light into its component colours by a prism. Each colour has a different wavelength and refracts differently.
Violet Indigo Blue Green Yellow Orange Red
VIBGYOR
Violet bends most, Red bends least
Why Different Bending?
Different wavelengths travel at slightly different speeds in glass. Violet (shortest λ) is slowed most → bends most. Red (longest λ) bends least.
Rainbow
  • Sunlight + water droplets = natural prism
  • Dispersion + TIR inside droplets
  • Red appears on outside (top), Violet inside
  • Seen when sun is behind observer
