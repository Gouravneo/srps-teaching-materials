Chemical Properties of Metals

NCERT Class 10 · Chapter 3 · Section 3.2

Metals react with oxygen, water, acids, and salt solutions. Reactivity depends on the metal's position in the reactivity series.

1️⃣ With Oxygen

Metal + O₂ → Metal Oxide (basic)
4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

2️⃣ With Water

Metal + H₂O → Metal hydroxide + H₂↑
2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂↑ Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂↑ 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂↑

3️⃣ With Dilute Acids

Metal + Acid → Salt + H₂↑ (hydrogen burns with pop)
Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑ Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂↑ Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂↑

4️⃣ With Salt Solutions

More reactive metal displaces less reactive metal
Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu↓ Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu↓
Blue CuSO₄ solution turns green (FeSO₄); red Cu deposits on iron nail

Chemical Properties of Non-metals

NCERT Class 10 · Chapter 3 · Section 3.3
Non-metal oxides are generally acidic · Non-metals do NOT react with dilute acids

With Oxygen → Acidic Oxides

C + O₂ → CO₂ S + O₂ → SO₂ 4P + 5O₂ → 2P₂O₅
CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid) → turns blue litmus red

With Water

Most non-metals do NOT react with water directly.

Exception — Chlorine:
Cl₂ + H₂O → HCl + HOCl
Chlorine water is acidic

With Dilute Acids

Generally, non-metals do NOT react with dilute acids.

They cannot displace hydrogen from acids (unlike metals).

With Metals → Ionic Compounds

2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl Mg + Cl₂ → MgCl₂ 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃
Electron transfer → ionic bonding

How Metals & Non-metals React — Ionic Bond

NaCl Formation · NCERT Class 10 Chapter 3 · Section 3.4

Na has 1 outer electron. Cl needs 1 more. Transfer happens →

Na loses 1 electron → Na⁺ (positive ion / cation)
Cl gains 1 electron → Cl⁻ (negative ion / anion)
Na⁺ and Cl⁻ attract each other → NaCl (sodium chloride = common table salt)
Na + Cl → Na⁺Cl⁻ (NaCl)

Properties of Ionic Compounds

  • High melting and boiling points
  • Solid at room temperature
  • Conduct electricity when dissolved in water or melted
  • Do NOT conduct in solid state
  • Generally soluble in water

Other Examples

MgCl₂ — Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻
CaCl₂ — Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻
Al₂O₃ — 2Al³⁺ + 3O²⁻
Metals form cations · Non-metals form anions

Occurrence and Extraction of Metals

NCERT Class 10 · Chapter 3 · Section 3.5
Mineral → Ore → Metal (after removing Gangue)
Mineral: naturally occurring compound · Ore: mineral from which metal can be profitably extracted · Gangue: impurities in ore
🔴 High Reactivity
K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al
Method: Electrolytic Reduction

Too reactive to be reduced by carbon.

Al₂O₃ → electrolysis of molten Al₂O₃ (Hall–Héroult process)

NaCl → electrolysis of molten NaCl
🟡 Medium Reactivity
Zn, Fe, Pb, Ni
Method: Reduction with Carbon

ZnO + C → Zn + CO
Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂

Iron extracted in a blast furnace using coke (C) and limestone
🟢 Low Reactivity
Cu, Hg, Au, Ag
Method: Found free OR simple heating

2HgS + 3O₂ → 2HgO + 2SO₂
2HgO → 2Hg + O₂

Gold found as free metal in river beds — Karnataka & AP rivers
Refining — Electrolytic Refining (for Cu, Zn, Sn, Ni, Ag, Au)
Impure metal = Anode · Pure metal = Cathode · Metal salt solution = Electrolyte
Current flows → impure metal dissolves from anode → pure metal deposits on cathode

Corrosion and Its Prevention

NCERT Class 10 · Chapter 3 · Section 3.6
Corrosion
When a metal is slowly eaten away / deteriorated by reaction with moisture, oxygen, or other substances in its environment.

Rusting of Iron

4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O
Fe₂O₃·xH₂O = Rust (hydrated iron oxide) = reddish-brown

Both oxygen AND moisture are needed for rusting.
Proof: dry iron does not rust; water without O₂ does not rust iron.

Other Types of Corrosion

🪙 Silver tarnish: Ag + H₂S → Ag₂S (black)
🟢 Copper patina: Cu → Cu(OH)₂·CuCO₃ (green)
⚪ Aluminium: Al₂O₃ layer forms → protects (self-healing)

Prevention of Corrosion

1️⃣ Painting — iron gates, bridges, buildings
2️⃣ Galvanising — coat iron with zinc (Zn bucket, roofing)
3️⃣ Electroplating — thin layer of non-reactive metal
4️⃣ Oiling / Greasing — machines, tools
5️⃣ Alloying — mix metals to prevent corrosion

Alloys

Alloy Composition Use
BrassCu + ZnUtensils, bells, fixtures
BronzeCu + SnCoins, medals, statues

Alloys have lower conductivity and higher hardness than pure metals · Pure gold (24K) is too soft for jewellery → alloyed with Ag/Cu

🇮🇳 Metals in Indian Daily Life

Real-world examples from Karnataka & India
💍

Gold Jewellery

Gold is least reactive → does not corrode → stays shiny for centuries.

22K gold (91.7% Au + Cu/Ag) — alloyed for strength for bangles and necklaces.
🍲

Aluminium Vessels

Pressure cookers, tiffin boxes — Al is light, good conductor, low cost.

Thin Al₂O₃ layer naturally protects the surface (self-healing oxide coat).
⚡

Copper Wiring

Copper is highly ductile and best conductor among common metals → used for all electrical wiring in homes, factories, transformers.
⛏️

NMDC Iron Ore (Karnataka)

Bellary-Hospet region has one of India's largest iron ore reserves.

Haematite (Fe₂O₃) — ore of iron — extracted in blast furnaces → steel.

Diwali Fireworks

🟢 Ba compounds = green

Iron Bridges (Rusting!)

Coastal bridges near Mangaluru, Goa need regular anti-rust painting — salty sea air accelerates rusting by providing electrolytes.

Chapter Summary

NCERT Class 10 Chemistry · Chapter 3 · Click to reveal each point
1. Physical Properties
Metals: lustrous, hard, malleable, ductile, good conductors, sonorous. Non-metals: dull, brittle, poor conductors. Exceptions: Na/K soft · Hg liquid · I₂ lustrous · graphite conducts.
2. Chemical Properties of Metals
React with O₂ → basic oxides · H₂O → hydroxide + H₂ · dilute acids → salt + H₂ · salt solutions → displacement. Cu, Au, Pt have very low reactivity.
3. Reactivity Series
K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au > Pt. More reactive displaces less reactive from salt solution. Determines extraction method.
4. Ionic Bond Formation
Metals lose electrons → cations. Non-metals gain electrons → anions. NaCl: Na loses 1e⁻ → Na⁺; Cl gains 1e⁻ → Cl⁻. Ionic compounds: high MP, conduct when dissolved.
5. Extraction of Metals
High reactivity: electrolytic reduction (Al). Medium: carbon reduction (Fe in blast furnace). Low: found free or by heating (Cu, Hg, Au). Refining: electrolytic refining.
6. Corrosion & Alloys
Rusting: Fe + O₂ + H₂O → Fe₂O₃·xH₂O. Prevention: painting, galvanising, alloying, oiling. Alloys: brass (Cu+Zn), bronze (Cu+Sn), steel (Fe+C), stainless steel (Fe+Cr+Ni).

🧠 Quick Quiz — Metals & Non-metals

Class 10 Chemistry · Chapter 3 · Click an option to answer

Q1. Which property is characteristic of non-metals but NOT metals?

A. Malleability
B. Ductility
C. Brittleness
D. Sonority
✅ Non-metals are brittle — sulphur, phosphorus crumble when hammered. Malleability, ductility, sonority are metal properties.

Q2. An iron nail is placed in copper sulphate solution. What is observed?

A. No change; iron does not react with salts
B. Iron displaces copper; blue solution turns greenish; copper deposits on nail
C. Copper displaces iron; solution turns dark blue
D. Both metals dissolve; solution becomes colourless
✅ Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. Fe is more reactive than Cu (higher in reactivity series) → displaces Cu. Blue solution (CuSO₄) turns light green (FeSO₄). Red copper metal deposits on iron nail.

Q3. Aluminium cannot be extracted by reducing its oxide with carbon because —

A. Al₂O₃ does not contain Al
B. Al is more reactive than carbon and cannot be displaced by it
C. Carbon reacts with aluminium to form a compound
D. Al₂O₃ has a very low melting point
✅ A metal can be displaced from its oxide only by a more reactive element. Aluminium (Al) is more reactive than carbon (C), so C cannot reduce Al₂O₃. Electrolytic reduction is used instead.

Q4. In NaCl formation, which electron transfer and ions are formed correctly?

A. Na gains 1 electron → Na⁻; Cl loses 1 electron → Cl⁺
B. Na loses 1 electron → Na⁺; Cl gains 1 electron → Cl⁻
C. Both Na and Cl share electrons equally
D. Na loses 2 electrons → Na²⁺; Cl gains 2 electrons → Cl²⁻
✅ Metals (Na) LOSE electrons → form positive ions (cations). Non-metals (Cl) GAIN electrons → form negative ions (anions). Na (2,8,1) loses 1e⁻ → Na⁺ (2,8). Cl (2,8,7) gains 1e⁻ → Cl⁻ (2,8,8). Both achieve noble gas configuration.
