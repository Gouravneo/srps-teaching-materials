SLIDE 3 · PHYSICAL PROPERTIES

Physical Properties of Metals

Lustre
Metals have a shiny surface when freshly cut/polished. Ex: iron, gold, copper shine.
Malleability
Can be beaten into thin sheets without breaking. Gold is the most malleable — 1g gold → 1 m² sheet.
Ductility
Can be drawn into thin wires. 1g gold → 2 km wire. Copper wiring in homes.
Conductivity
Good conductors of heat and electricity. Silver is best conductor; copper is most used.
Sonority
Produce a ringing/metallic sound when struck. School bell = metal.

SLIDE 5 · CHEMICAL PROPERTIES OF METALS

Metals Reacting with Oxygen

General Rule
Metal + O₂ → Metal Oxide
Metal oxides are basic in nature
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO ← white ash, bright flame 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O ← vigorous, explosive 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃ ← protective layer 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO ← black coating
MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ — basic · turns litmus blue
All metal oxides dissolve in water to give alkaline (basic) solutions
Amphoteric oxides: ZnO and Al₂O₃ react with both acids AND bases
ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O
ZnO + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂O

SLIDE 7 · CHEMICAL PROPERTIES OF METALS

Metals Reacting with Dilute Acids

General Rule
Metal + Dilute Acid → Salt + Hydrogen gas↑
Hydrogen burns with a 'pop' sound when a burning splint is brought near
Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑ Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂↑ Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂↑ 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂↑
Important: Copper, silver, gold do NOT react with dilute HCl or H₂SO₄
They are below hydrogen in the reactivity series.
Hydrogen gas test: Bring a burning splint near the mouth of the test tube. H₂ burns with a 'pop' sound.
💡 Nitric acid (HNO₃) is a special case — it reacts differently and does NOT release H₂ gas from metals.
SLIDE 8 · DISPLACEMENT REACTIONS

Metals Reacting with Salt Solutions

Displacement Reaction
A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt solution.
This is the basis of the Reactivity Series.
Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu↓
Iron nail in blue CuSO₄ solution:
🔵 Blue solution turns green (FeSO₄ forms)
🔴 Red-brown copper metal deposits on iron nail
Fe is more reactive than Cu → displaces it
Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu↓
Zinc (more reactive than Cu) displaces copper. Blue solution becomes colourless (ZnSO₄).
Cu + FeSO₄ → No reaction ✗
Copper is less reactive than iron → CANNOT displace iron from its salt.
Cu + ZnSO₄ → No reaction ✗
Copper is less reactive than zinc → cannot displace zinc.
💡 Activity 3.4 (NCERT): Place iron nail in CuSO₄ solution for 20 min. Observe colour change from blue to light green and copper deposit on nail.

"King Na Ca Mg Al · Zn Fe Pb · Cu Hg Ag Au Pt"

SLIDE 10 · CHEMICAL PROPERTIES OF NON-METALS

Chemical Properties of Non-metals

With Oxygen
Non-metals + O₂ → Non-metal oxide (acidic)
C + O₂ → CO₂ (acidic oxide) S + O₂ → SO₂ (acidic oxide) 4P + 5O₂ → 2P₂O₅
CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid) — turns blue litmus red
SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃ (sulphurous acid)
With Water
Most non-metals do NOT react directly. Exception: Cl₂
Cl₂ + H₂O → HCl + HOCl
With Acids
Generally, non-metals do NOT react with dilute acids (unlike metals — no H₂ produced)
With Metals → Ionic Compounds
This is the most important reaction! Electron transfer occurs.
2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl Mg + Cl₂ → MgCl₂ 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃
⚠️ Key comparison:
Metal oxides → BASIC (turn litmus blue)
Non-metal oxides → ACIDIC (turn litmus red)
ZnO, Al₂O₃ → AMPHOTERIC (react with both)
SLIDE 11 · IONIC BOND FORMATION

How do Metals and Non-metals React?

Ionic (Electrovalent) Bond
A chemical bond formed by the transfer of electrons from a metal atom to a non-metal atom, creating oppositely charged ions that attract each other.
⬆️
Metals LOSE electrons
Form positive ions
= CATIONS
Na → Na⁺ + e⁻
⚡
Electron Transfer
Electrons move from metal to non-metal.
Both atoms achieve noble gas configuration (8 outer electrons)
⬇️
Non-metals GAIN electrons
Form negative ions
= ANIONS
Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻
Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl · The oppositely charged ions attract each other → ionic lattice
💡 Valence electrons determine how many electrons a metal loses or non-metal gains. Na (Group 1) loses 1; Mg (Group 2) loses 2; Al (Group 13) loses 3.
SLIDE 12 · IONIC BOND — NaCl EXAMPLE

Formation of NaCl (Sodium Chloride)

Sodium (Na)
Electronic configuration: 2, 8, 1
Has 1 electron in outermost shell → loses it easily → becomes Na⁺ (2, 8)
Chlorine (Cl)
Electronic configuration: 2, 8, 7
Needs 1 more electron in outer shell → gains it → becomes Cl⁻ (2, 8, 8)
NaCl (result)
Na⁺ and Cl⁻ attract each other by electrostatic force → form ionic compound NaCl
Na + Cl → Na⁺Cl⁻ (NaCl)
Na (2,8,1) → Na⁺ (2,8) · Cl (2,8,7) → Cl⁻ (2,8,8) · Both now have noble gas configuration!
SLIDE 13 · IONIC COMPOUNDS

Properties of Ionic Compounds

  • Physical state: Solid at room temperature (strong ionic lattice holds them)
  • Melting / Boiling points: Very high (strong electrostatic forces between ions)
  • Conductivity in solid state: Do NOT conduct electricity (ions are fixed in lattice)
  • Conductivity in solution / melt: Conduct electricity (ions are free to move)
  • Solubility: Generally soluble in water (polar solvent)
  • Insoluble in: Organic solvents like petrol, kerosene
Ionic CompoundIonsExample Use
NaClNa⁺, Cl⁻Common salt, food
MgCl₂Mg²⁺, 2Cl⁻Fire retardants
CaCl₂Ca²⁺, 2Cl⁻Drying agent
Al₂O₃2Al³⁺, 3O²⁻Extraction of Al
CaOCa²⁺, O²⁻Quicklime
💡 Ionic compounds conduct electricity because current = flow of charged particles (ions). In solid, ions are immobile. When dissolved or melted, ions move freely → current flows.
SLIDE 14 · OCCURRENCE OF METALS

Occurrence of Metals in Nature

Mineral
Any naturally occurring substance in the earth's crust that contains a metal. Not all minerals can be profitably used. Ex: bauxite, haematite, calamine
Ore
A mineral from which a metal can be extracted profitably. All ores are minerals, but not all minerals are ores.
Gangue
The unwanted impurities (sand, mud, soil) mixed with the ore. Must be removed before extraction.

SLIDE 15 · EXTRACTION OF METALS

Extraction: Metals of Low Reactivity

Metals: Cu, Hg, Ag, Au, Pt
Low reactivity → found free OR require very little energy to extract
Method: Found Free / Simple Heating
These metals are either found as free elements (Au, Pt) or their ores decompose on simple heating (roasting)
Mercury from Cinnabar (HgS):
2HgS + 3O₂ → 2HgO + 2SO₂ 2HgO →(heat)→ 2Hg + O₂
Step 1: Roasting in air → HgO
Step 2: HgO decomposes on heating → liquid mercury
Gold:
Found as free metal in riverbeds (Hutti Gold Mines, Karnataka — one of India's oldest gold mines!)
SLIDE 16 · EXTRACTION OF METALS

Extraction: Metals of Medium Reactivity — Iron

Metals: Zn, Fe, Pb, Ni, Sn
Method: Convert ore → Oxide → Reduce with carbon/CO
Step 1 — Convert Ore to Metal Oxide
Roasting (sulfide ores → heat in air):
2ZnS + 3O₂ →(heat in air)→ 2ZnO + 2SO₂↑
Calcination (carbonate ores → heat without air):
ZnCO₃ →(heat, no air)→ ZnO + CO₂↑
Step 2 — Reduce Oxide using Carbon (Coke):
ZnO + C → Zn + CO   (coke acts as reducing agent)
Extraction of Iron — Blast Furnace
Raw materials: Haematite (Fe₂O₃) + Coke (C) + Limestone (CaCO₃)
C + O₂ → CO₂ (combustion of coke) CO₂ + C → 2CO (CO forms — reducing agent) Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ (iron reduced) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (limestone decomposes) CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag forms)
Slag (CaSiO₃) — floats above molten iron, removed separately
Pig iron — impure iron tapped from bottom of furnace
Steel — pig iron + controlled carbon + other metals
SLIDE 17 · EXTRACTION OF METALS

Extraction: Metals of High Reactivity — Aluminium

Metals: K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al
Too reactive to be reduced by carbon.
Method: Electrolytic Reduction
Extraction of Aluminium (Hall–Héroult Process)
Ore: Bauxite (Al₂O₃·2H₂O)
Step 1: Purification — Al₂O₃ (alumina) is obtained from bauxite
Step 2: Electrolysis of molten Al₂O₃ (mixed with cryolite to lower melting point)
Anode: Carbon (graphite) — O₂ released, burns anode
Cathode: Carbon lining — Al deposits
Al₂O₃ →(electricity)→ 2Al + 3/2 O₂ At cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al At anode: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻
⚠️ Carbon cannot reduce Al₂O₃ because Al is more reactive than C in the activity series. Only electrical energy (electrolysis) can extract it.
SLIDE 18 · REFINING OF METALS

Electrolytic Refining

Electrolytic Refining
Method used to purify impure metals. Metals refined: Cu, Zn, Sn, Ni, Ag, Au
Anode (+) = Impure metal (dissolves)
Cathode (−) = Pure metal (deposits)
Electrolyte = Salt solution of the metal (e.g., CuSO₄ for copper)
Process:
1. Impure copper dissolves from anode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻
2. Cu²⁺ ions move through electrolyte
3. Pure copper deposits at cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
4. Impurities (Fe, Zn, Ni etc.) remain at bottom as anode mud
💡 Anode mud may contain precious metals like gold and silver — valuable byproduct! This is how silver and gold are recovered during copper refining.
SLIDE 19 · CORROSION

Corrosion of Metals

Corrosion
The slow deterioration (eating away) of metals due to attack by moisture, oxygen, acids or other substances in the environment.
Rusting of Iron
4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O
Fe₂O₃·xH₂O = Rust (hydrated iron oxide) — reddish brown
Both oxygen AND moisture are essential for rusting.
Proof with 3 test tubes:
1. Fe in dry air → No rust
2. Fe in boiled water (no O₂) → No rust
3. Fe in normal water with air → Rusts!
💡 Coastal areas (Mangaluru, Goa) — salty seawater accelerates rusting because dissolved salts increase conductivity, speeding up the electrochemical corrosion process.
Other Types of Corrosion
MetalProductAppearance
IronFe₂O₃·xH₂OReddish brown (rust)
SilverAg₂SBlack tarnish
CopperCu(OH)₂·CuCO₃Green patina (verdigris)
AluminiumAl₂O₃ (thin layer)Dull surface but protective!
Self-protecting metals: Al forms a thin, continuous Al₂O₃ layer that prevents further oxidation. This is called passivation. Similarly, chromium (in stainless steel) is self-protecting.
SLIDE 20 · PREVENTION OF CORROSION

Prevention of Corrosion

1. Painting
Coat the metal surface with paint — blocks contact with oxygen and moisture.

Ex: Bridges, iron gates, railway tracks repainted every few years.
2. Galvanising
Coat iron with a thin layer of zinc (Zn) by dipping in molten zinc or electroplating.

Zn is more reactive than Fe → corrodes first, protecting iron. Even if Zn coat is scratched, it still protects!

Ex: Zinc buckets, roofing sheets, GI pipes.
3. Electroplating
Deposit a thin layer of a non-reactive metal (Cr, Ni, Ag, Au) using electrolysis.

Ex: Chromium plating on car parts, gold plating on jewellery, tin plating on food cans.
4. Oiling / Greasing
Apply oil or grease to metal surfaces — prevents contact with moisture.

Ex: Machine parts, hinges, bicycles, guns.
5. Alloying
Mix iron with chromium and nickel → stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni) — does not rust!

Used in kitchen utensils, surgical instruments, industrial equipment.
6. Tinning
Coat iron with tin (Sn) — food cans ("tin cans") are actually tin-plated iron. Tin is non-toxic and protects from corrosion.
SLIDE 21 · ALLOYS

Alloys — Mixtures of Metals

Alloy
A homogeneous mixture of two or more metals (or a metal and a non-metal). Alloys are made to improve properties such as hardness, strength, or corrosion resistance.
AlloyCompositionPropertiesUses
BrassCu + Zn (20–45% Zn)Hard, corrosion-resistantUtensils, musical instruments, taps
BronzeCu + Sn (10–20% Sn)Hard, resists corrosionCoins, medals, statues, bells
SteelFe + C (0.2–2% C)Hard, strongConstruction, machinery, tools
Stainless SteelFe + Cr(18%) + Ni(8%)Hard, non-rustingSurgical tools, kitchenware
SolderPb + Sn (50:50)Low melting pointJoining electrical wires
AmalgamHg + other metalPastyDental fillings
Why are alloys better than pure metals?
  • Higher hardness and strength
  • Lower electrical conductivity (used in heating elements)
  • Higher corrosion resistance
  • Lower melting point (for soldering)
💡 Pure iron is too soft and easily rusted. Steel (Fe + C) is much stronger. Stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni) does not rust at all. Adding even a small % of Cr completely changes iron's properties!
Gold Purity:
24 carat = 100% pure gold (too soft for jewellery)
22 carat = 91.7% Au + Cu/Ag (most jewellery)
18 carat = 75% Au (used in some jewellery)

SLIDE 23 · COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

Common Misconceptions & Corrections

Common MisconceptionCorrect Understanding
All metals are solids at room temperature Mercury (Hg) is a liquid metal at room temperature
Non-metals cannot conduct electricity Graphite (an allotrope of carbon) conducts electricity. It is used in pencils and as electrode in batteries
Rusting happens with any moisture Both oxygen AND water (moisture) are essential for rusting. Dry iron does not rust; water without dissolved O₂ does not rust iron
All metal oxides are insoluble Na₂O, CaO, MgO dissolve in water to form alkaline solutions (NaOH, Ca(OH)₂, Mg(OH)₂)
Galvanising = painting with Zn paint Galvanising = coating iron with metallic zinc by dipping in molten Zn or electroplating. Not a paint.
Ionic compounds always dissolve in water "Generally soluble" — some ionic compounds like BaSO₄, CaCO₃ are insoluble. Solubility rules apply.
Alloys are compounds Alloys are mixtures (homogeneous), not compounds. The metals do not chemically bond — they are physically mixed.
SLIDE 24 · CHAPTER SUMMARY

Chapter 3 Summary — Metals and Non-metals

Physical Properties: Metals — lustrous, malleable, ductile, conductor, sonorous. Non-metals — dull, brittle, insulator. Exceptions: Na/K soft, Hg liquid, I₂ lustrous, graphite conducts.
Chemical Properties: Metals react with O₂ (basic oxides), H₂O (hydroxide + H₂), dilute acids (salt + H₂), salt solutions (displacement). Non-metals form acidic oxides.
Reactivity Series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au > Pt. More reactive displaces less reactive.
Ionic Bond: Metal loses e⁻ → cation · Non-metal gains e⁻ → anion · Electrostatic attraction = ionic bond. NaCl: Na (2,8,1) → Na⁺; Cl (2,8,7) → Cl⁻. Ionic compounds: high MP, conduct when dissolved.
Extraction: High reactivity → electrolytic reduction (Al). Medium → carbon reduction (Fe in blast furnace). Low → free/simple heating (Au, Hg). Refining → electrolytic refining.
Corrosion: Fe + O₂ + H₂O → Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust). Prevention: painting, galvanising (Zn), electroplating, alloying. Ag tarnishes (Ag₂S). Cu turns green (Cu(OH)₂·CuCO₃).
Alloys: Brass (Cu+Zn), Bronze (Cu+Sn), Steel (Fe+C), Stainless steel (Fe+Cr+Ni), Solder (Pb+Sn). Alloys have better properties than pure metals.
💡 Karnataka connection: Bellary-Hospet iron ore (haematite) · Hutti gold mines (Au found free) · Aluminium from Vedanta (Lanjigarh, nearby state) · Copper wiring in BESCOM electrical grid
