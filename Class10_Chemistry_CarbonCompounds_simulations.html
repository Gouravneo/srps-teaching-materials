⚛️ Covalent Bond Formation
Watch atoms share electrons to form stable molecules
🔵Covalent bond = atoms SHARE electrons. No transfer — both atoms benefit by getting a full outer shell.
1️⃣Single bond (—): share 1 electron pair. Example: H–H, C–H, C–C in alkanes.
2️⃣Double bond (=): share 2 electron pairs. Example: C=C in ethene, C=O in CO₂. More reactive.
3️⃣Triple bond (≡): share 3 electron pairs. Example: C≡C in ethyne, N≡N. Very short, strong bond.
4️⃣Carbon forms exactly 4 bonds (tetravalency). It can bond with H, O, N, halogens and other C atoms.
Single bond: weakest Double: reactive Triple: strongest

🔥 Combustion of Carbon Compounds
Carbon compounds burn to release heat energy
🔵Complete combustion (excess O₂): blue flame, products are CO₂ + H₂O. Maximum energy released. Safe.
🟡Incomplete combustion (limited O₂): yellow/sooty flame, products are CO + soot. Less energy. CO is toxic!
⚠️CO (carbon monoxide) is colourless, odourless, and deadly. It binds to haemoglobin, preventing oxygen transport.
Select a fuel to see the equation
Blue flame → complete Yellow flame → incomplete CO → toxic gas

🧪 Bromine Water Test for Unsaturation
A quick lab test to identify double/triple bonds
🟤Bromine water (Br₂ in water) is brown in colour. It reacts with unsaturated bonds.
✅Alkenes/Alkynes → decolourize bromine water (brown → colourless). The double/triple bond reacts with Br₂ (addition reaction).
❌Alkanes → NO change. Brown colour stays. Alkanes have only single bonds — cannot react with Br₂ at room temperature without light.
CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂Br–CH₂Br
(ethene + bromine → 1,2-dibromoethane)
Brown stays → saturated Colourless → unsaturated
🔴 Oxidation of Ethanol
Flask contains ethanol (C₂H₅OH). Start by adding the oxidising agent.
🔴 Ethanol → Ethanoic Acid
Oxidation reaction using KMnO₄ or K₂Cr₂O₇
🔴KMnO₄ (potassium permanganate) is a strong oxidising agent. It is purple/violet in colour and gets reduced (decolourizes) during the reaction.
🍷This is the same reaction that happens when wine turns to vinegar — ethanol in wine gets oxidised to ethanoic acid by bacteria in air.
🧪Also done industrially using K₂Cr₂O₇ (orange → green when reduced).
C₂H₅OH + [O] → CH₃COOH
(Ethanol + Oxygen → Ethanoic acid)
KMnO₄: purple → colourless Product: vinegar smell

🧼 Soap and Micelle Formation
How soap removes grease from surfaces
🧴Saponification: Fat/Oil + NaOH → Soap + Glycerol. NaOH → hard soap. KOH → liquid soap.
🔵Soap molecule has two parts: a long hydrophobic tail (–CH₂CH₂CH₂–) and a hydrophilic ionic head (–COO⁻Na⁺).
⚽Micelle: when soap meets grease, tails point inward into grease, heads face water → spherical cage around grease droplet.
💧The micelle is water-soluble because its outer surface (ionic heads) is hydrophilic. It washes away with water carrying the grease!
⚠️Hard water problem: Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ in hard water react with soap → insoluble scum. Use detergent instead!
Tail → hydrophobic Head → hydrophilic Micelle → water-soluble
